Incident response playbooks and runbooks

Twelve playbooks in the NIST SP 800-61 format, tied to the detection rules and improved after every incident.

Why playbooks

At 3 a.m., nobody should have to improvise. A playbook tells the analyst on duty what to check, what to contain and who to call, for one specific type of incident.

What each playbook contains

I follow the NIST SP 800-61r3 and CSF 2.0 structure: the detection rules involved, indicators of compromise, detection, triage, containment, eradication, recovery, post-incident review, escalation, and the time to detect and to respond (MTTD and MTTR).

Runbooks

Alongside the playbooks, I write runbooks for the routine work: the exact steps to run a task the same way every time, whoever is on duty.

Continuous improvement

After every real incident, the post-incident review asks what slowed us down. The answer goes back into the playbook, and often into a new or better detection rule. Tabletop exercises with the team test the playbooks before a real incident does.
